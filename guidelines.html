Guidelines & Protocols

Exam-day protocol, how to use your resources, how to revise, a daily do & don't checklist and a 7-day emergency plan. Exam patterns change, so always confirm the final details in the official bulletin.

Exam-day protocol

The night before (all exams)

  • Pack: admit card (2 printouts), original photo ID, permitted pen / ID proofs as per the bulletin. Leave electronics and notes behind.
  • Visit or map the centre, plan the route and a backup route. Aim to reach 60–90 minutes before reporting time.
  • Stop new topics. Skim your Mistake Notebook and RR notes for 45 minutes at most, then sleep 7+ hours.

In the exam hall (all exams)

  • Spend the first 2 minutes on instructions and the interface: navigation, mark-for-review, rough-sheet rules.
  • Pass 1: answer what you know. Pass 2: mark-and-return for the doubtful ones. Pass 3: educated guesses where the exam rewards them.
  • Check the clock at fixed milestones (every 50 questions). Don't change an answer unless you can state a concrete reason.
NEET PG
  • Format: computer-based MCQs, 180 questions, +4 for a correct answer and −1 for a wrong one.
  • Strategy: negative marking is real, so skip an item when you can't narrow it to two options. Two-option guesses are worth taking.
  • Time: budget roughly 50–60 seconds per question and bank time on image/one-liner questions for the long clinical vignettes.
  • Subjects first: clear Medicine, Surgery, OBG, Paediatrics, PSM, Pathology, Pharmacology and Microbiology confidently. They carry the bulk of the paper.
Question count, duration and slot rules are set by the NBEMS bulletin for your attempt. Read it once, fully, a week before.
INICET
  • Format: computer-based, 200-question pattern with +1 / −⅓ marking (as used in this app).
  • Strategy: a −⅓ penalty means a guess between two options is positive expected value. Don't leave answerable questions blank.
  • Content: INICET leans on detailed, reference-level facts and images. Spend your last fortnight on PYQs, images and high-yield tables.
  • Pace: aim for a first pass finished with 20–25 minutes spare for flagged questions.
Check the AIIMS bulletin for the current paper pattern, duration and reporting rules.
FMGE
  • Format: 300 MCQs in two sessions of 150 (2.5 hours each), 1 mark per correct answer, no negative marking. You need 150/300 (50%) to pass.
  • Strategy: never leave a blank. Eliminate and guess.
  • Between sessions: eat, hydrate and don't re-litigate session 1. Treat session 2 as a fresh exam.
  • Priority: PSM, Medicine, Surgery and OBG (~30 each) form the backbone. The pass mark is about being solid everywhere, not brilliant in one subject.
UPSC CMS
  • Format: two papers of 120 questions, 2 hours each (Paper I: Medicine + Paediatrics, Paper II: Surgery, OBG, PSM). One-third negative marking, no calculators, no scribe.
  • Strategy: because both papers are scored separately for qualifying, don't let one weak paper drag you. Pace each paper independently.
  • Prep emphasis: use the PYTS page. CMS repeats topics heavily across years.
  • Personality test: after clearing the written stage, keep your DAF, clinical reasoning and current health-policy basics ready.
Resource usage protocol

Main notes (first pass, R1)

  • One primary source per subject. Read actively: after each section close the page and write 3 recall points.
  • Annotate the margin with the Qbank question that tested the idea. This is what makes revision fast later.
  • Tick R1 in the Topic Library only when you can explain the topic without looking.

RR (rapid revision) notes & videos

  • RR is for R2 and R3, never R1. It compresses what you already understand. It can't teach what you haven't.
  • One RR cycle = one subject in one sitting. Follow it with 20–40 MCQs from that subject the same day.

Qbanks

  • Do subject-wise timed blocks of 20–40 Qs. Review every question, right or wrong, within 24 hours.
  • Log every wrong or lucky-right item in the Mistake Notebook with the micro-topic. Re-read the notebook weekly.
  • Tag the source on each log so you can see which Qbank is giving you the weakest accuracy.

Avoid

  • Resource-hopping. Three resources revised thrice beat ten resources read once.
  • Passive video marathons without recall practice.
Revision strategy: spaced repetition

Memory fades along a curve, and each well-timed recall flattens it. Spaced repetition means revisiting a topic just as you're about to forget it, so fewer revisions give longer retention.

A practical schedule

  1. Day 0: first learning (R1 ✓).
  2. Day 1–2: 10-minute recall from memory plus 10 MCQs.
  3. Day 7: second pass from RR notes (R2 ✓).
  4. Day 21–30: third pass focused on mistakes and images (R3 ✓).
  5. Final 3 weeks: lock topics 🔒 you can answer cold. Keep only shaky topics in rotation.

Principles

  • Active recall beats re-reading. Cover the page, retrieve, then check.
  • Interleave subjects within a week so retrieval stays effortful.
  • Lock honestly. A topic is locked only if you got it right in the last two Qbank attempts.
